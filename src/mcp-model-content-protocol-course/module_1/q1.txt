Applying the MCP evaluation framework fundamentally shifts the decision-making process from a narrow technical comparison to a holistic, risk-managed business decision.

* **Granular Architectural Realism:** Rather than viewing all healthcare integrations through a single lens, scoring individual data sources—from static pharmaceutical APIs (complexity score 2) to high-frequency streaming patient monitors (complexity score 5)—highlighted that custom connectors fail primarily due to maintenance fragmentation rather than initial protocol setup.


* **Compliance as an Enabler, Not a Bottleneck:** In healthcare, security often stalls adoption. Evaluating MCP's client/server architecture demonstrated that standardized context tools and centralized logging actually simplify HIPAA audits compared to maintaining separate custom security implementations across five distinct codebases.


* **Quantifiable ROI Over Technical Hype:** Framing the decision around concrete engineering metrics (reducing monthly maintenance hours from ~120 to ~30) proved that a standardized protocol achieves full financial break-even within 4 months, making the executive case for the CTO compelling and low-risk.


* **De-risking Adoption via Hybrid Execution:** The framework made it clear that adopting a standardized protocol doesn't require a risky "big bang" rewrite. A phased rollout—starting with low-complexity targets before tackling real-time telemetry—addresses developer learning curves while preserving system stability for life-critical clinical decision support.
